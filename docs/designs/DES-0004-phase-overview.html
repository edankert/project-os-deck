The phase overview, round four

The wheel now holds only the phases still to finish. Finished phases sit on a shelf under it, the backlog has a tray of its own, and tickets that name no phase get a suggested phase. The last two plates show the same overview, and a phase page, as a Glass desk with every ticket as a card.

What changed since round three

  • Finished phases leave the wheel. A done or superseded phase has nothing left to steer, so it gives up its sector and sits on a shelf under the wheel, one small disc each, in phase order. A disc keeps one mark: a violet dot when acceptance tests in that phase are owed again.
  • "Owed again" is a new state. An acceptance test whose pass was withdrawn by a later change is neither unfinished work nor a failure; it is a retest the next release needs. It is violet everywhere.
  • The backlog is a tray, not a phase. A phase marked as the backlog (your-trainer's PHASE-999 Future) sits in its own strip, grouped by type, with no exit criteria and no completion figure.
  • Tickets with no phase get a suggested one. Deck reads which tickets each one links to and when it was created, and proposes a phase. You accept a group at a time. The centre of the wheel shows what is left after that.
  • MATRIX lists the finished phases rather than giving each a row of bars, and the same for the backlog.
  • Glass. Two plates show the overview as a Glass field you stand inside, and a phase page as a Glass desk.

GLASS: THE OVERVIEW

Standing inside the wheel: phases around you, depth is state, every ticket a card

The wheel becomes the Glass field, seen from its centre. Each phase still to finish is a direction you can face; the compass across the top shows them in wheel order, with the selected phase straight ahead and its neighbours at the edges. Turning (the mouse wheel or the arrow keys, as in Glass today) brings the next phase round.

Depth is state, as Glass already treats priority. Failed and owed-again tests and tickets in progress stand in the front band as full cards. Open tickets fill the middle band. Closed tickets sit in the quiet band at the horizon as small tiles that can still be reached. The rings' order from the centre outwards becomes height, so requirements and features stand above tasks, tests and issues in every band.

Behind you are the finished phases and the backlog, exactly where Glass already keeps what is out of the way. The exit criteria travel with the phase as one card at the front left. Selecting any card lifts it onto the desk with its neighbourhood, which is FEAT-0017's behaviour unchanged.

7

GLASS: A PHASE PAGE

The phase page laid out as a Glass desk: the phase note, one collection per ticket type, and an open ticket

The phase page is a scene, not a page. The phase note opens as a full document on the left, with its exit criteria in it. Each ticket type with open tickets is a collection, using the three forms FEAT-0022 already has: cards when there are a few, a stack when they are all one kind of work, a table when there are many. Closed tickets stay in the collection, folded into one line.

Opening a ticket puts it on the desk as a document with its links around it (FEAT-0017), so following a test to the feature it covers, or an issue to its task, never leaves the phase. The arrangement can be saved as a named scene (FEAT-0023), so "PHASE-021 retest" can be reopened next week with live content.
