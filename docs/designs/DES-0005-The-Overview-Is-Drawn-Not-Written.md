# The overview is drawn, not written

**Deck's overview is now seven screens that show progress as pictures, and each one is drawn by the code Deck runs.** The wheel of phases opens Deck. Behind it are a phase, a ticket and re-phasing. Three more screens are where a person accepts a design, records an acceptance test and accepts a feature. Every plate below was drawn by the same functions Deck uses, from the real tickets of Deck and of your-trainer, so the design and the build cannot disagree. Edwin is asked to accept or decline it.

The interactive page is [DES-0005-the-overview.html](DES-0005-the-overview.html). It switches between the two workspaces and the seven screens, and pointing at any mark shows what it is. Served by the cockpit's viewer, a press on a sector, a ticket or a gate opens that screen, as in Deck. It is generated by `desktop/scripts/overview-plates.mjs`; nothing in it is drawn by hand.

> [!note] Revised and redrawn, 2026-10-15
> Edwin ran the built overview on 2026-10-15 and asked for five changes (under Revisions). The plates below are redrawn from the revised screens. Where a screen lists tickets as rows, the plate draws the rows flat; the pictures marked "in Deck" are taken from a real Deck window, where the rows stand on a tilted plane.

## Problem

**The Glass screens in DES-0004 show tickets as cards with titles, so reading them means reading text.** Plate 6 there shows 13 cards with truncated titles and an exit-criteria card of eight sentences. Plate 7 is a document, a list of features and a table of tests. Edwin's verdict on 2026-10-14: too text heavy, and nothing like Minority Report.

DES-0004's wheel works the other way. A glance says which phase is mostly done and where the open work is, and it carries no sentence. This design draws every screen that way, including three nobody had drawn: accepting a design, running an acceptance test and accepting a ticket.

**After using it, Edwin wanted tickets to look the same as in the cockpit, and to read them where reading is the point** (2026-10-15). A phase's tickets and a ticket's neighbours are lists, so they are drawn as the cockpit's rows, with an id, a title and a status each. A ticket's own screen shows its text.

## The look

The rules are in [[PHASE-0005-Overview]], "The look". Four of them are checked by code on every screen, on both workspaces, in `desktop/src/shared/overview-checks.ts`: at most 60 words at rest outside a list of tickets, every count drawn by its own mark, an address that names its screen, and a list twin that holds every drawn ticket and every control. The word count under each plate is that check's.

- **Colour is the ticket's type, as in the cockpit.** Features are blue, tasks green, requirements purple, issues red, risks orange, tests a lighter green, decisions teal and designs grey-blue. These are the cockpit's own colours (`--type-*` in its `cockpit.css`).
- **The fill is the state, as in the cockpit's overview.** Filled is done. Hollow is not started. Inverted and pulsing is in progress. Inverted and still is "complete, not proven". Solid with a slit is "resolved, nothing delivered". Hollow with a strike is "parked". A red dot on the corner is "needs you", whatever the type. On a wedge of the wheel, which is not a square, the same states are drawn as a full, hollow, half or dashed wedge, and "needs you" as a red outline that glows. The fill and the dot are worked out by the cockpit's own rules, ported into Deck and checked against the cockpit's answers: no ticket differs on either workspace ([[TASK-0144]]).
- **The state colours are still there to compare.** On the wheel and the re-phase screen, the ◐ button beside the list button, or the C key, redraws the wheel, its shelf and tray, and the re-phase cloud in DES-0004's state colours: closed green, in progress blue, failed red, owed violet, open amber, dropped grey. Lists of tickets, a ticket's own square and the gate keep the cockpit's marks in both. The colouring is part of the screen's address (`colour=state`), so a copied address shows the same picture; Deck remembers the last choice only for opening the wheel.
- **Shape is kind on the wheel and in the re-phase cloud.** Requirements are diamonds, features hexagons, tasks circles, tests squares, issues triangles, decisions and designs pentagons, risks octagons. In a list every ticket is the cockpit's square.
- **Needs you says what to do.** A ticket that needs you carries the cockpit's reason and one sentence: "Decide: take it into the backlog, or decline it" at triage, "Review it, then accept it or send it back" at review, "Run this test: it has never been run" for a test never run, "It failed: fix it, or run it again", or "Waiting on TASK-0002: finish that first". S3 shows it as a red banner with a button to where you act, S2 under the ticket's row, and every callout at its end.
- **The instrument, in 3D.** A dark field with a faint grid, corner brackets and a ticked bezel. Every screen stands on a plane the camera looks at: the wheel, re-phasing and the release test lean back, a phase leans a little, and a ticket, a design and a feature's gate start flat because they hold text to read. Drag with the middle button to turn and tilt, add Shift to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom toward the pointer, and double-click on empty space or press 0 to put it back; + and − zoom too. A list of tickets also stands on its own plane tilted away and leans toward the pointer. Moving between screens animates every ticket that is on both. Nothing moves for a person who asked for less motion, except by their own hand.
- **Words appear on demand.** Pointing at a mark or a row shows its callout in the cockpit's words, for example "FEAT-0024 Evidence stands beside the claim it supports (review) — needs you". L opens the list twin: the same screen as rows, for the keyboard and a screen reader.
- **Back and forward.** ← and → beside the Deck name, ⌘[ and ⌘], and the mouse's own buttons retrace the screens visited, as a browser does. Escape still goes up a level, from a ticket to its phase and from a phase to the wheel.

### What the gate's five parts count

A feature's gate has five parts, and each one counts a different kind of evidence. These meanings appear in the callout when a person points at a part; they are not written on the screen at rest (Edwin, 2026-10-15: no extra text on the gate).

- **Requirements**: the acceptance criteria of the requirements the feature implements, ticked with evidence.
- **Tasks**: the feature's tasks, done.
- **Reviews**: the independent reviews the feature needs (normally two), approved and recorded.
- **Automated tests**: tests with a command, with a result recorded.
- **Acceptance tests**: manual checks a person runs, with a pass in the release ledger.

A part Deck cannot read is drawn as "not reported". A part with nothing in it is drawn as "nothing to check". Neither is drawn as needing anyone.

## Level 2: height is time, a real 3D wheel to judge

**This is a design to look at, not something Deck draws.** It answers Edwin's question of 2026-10-15, whether 3D could carry more information. Each phase is a column standing on the ring, and its tickets rise up the column by the date their note was created, like the rings of a tree. So the picture says when the work happened as well as where it stands. A ticket is its type's colour, solid when it is done or under way and a wire frame when it is not started. What needs you is larger, glows red, and sends a beam up the full height, so it can be found from any angle. The column in the middle holds the tickets with no phase. Faint rings up the height mark the months.

## Towers on the ring, the wheel in 3D grouped by phase

**This is the design Edwin chose on 2026-10-16, drawn first as a page to judge and then built into Deck the same day: the wheel as towers standing on a ring.** He asked for a disc that is 3D, with its items stacked, grouped by feature inside each phase, and with the work in progress lit so it is clear what comes next. He also found the phase screen's web did not work. The page came first ([[TASK-0163]]); after turning it, Edwin asked for it to be built as suggested, and Deck now draws it ([[TASK-0164]] to [[TASK-0168]]).

- **Each unfinished phase is a raised segment of the ring.** Planned phases are low and dim, active ones raised and lit, and the phase in focus is highest, brightest, and marked ▶ with its name in cyan. The page opens turned so the focus phase faces you.
- **Each feature stands as a tower in its phase.** The feature itself is the tower's foot. Each floor above holds two of its tickets: its tasks, tests, requirements and designs, done at the bottom, then in progress, then not started, and what needs you on top. A tall tower is a big feature, and a solid one is far along. Which feature a ticket belongs to comes from its note's `parent:`, followed up through a task's parent, from the feature's own lists of tests, requirements and designs, or from the `tests:` of a task that belongs to the feature. A ticket that several features name stands in the lowest-numbered one's tower. The notes are read as they were at the commit the fixture was taken from, like the focus; for your-trainer, whose fixture was taken with uncommitted notes, a note the commit lacks is read from today's file.
- **The marks are the cockpit's**: the type colour, DES-0004's fills (solid done, half filled in progress, outline not started, dashed for resolved or parked), and the red needs-you dot on a floor. A tower with something that needs you wears a red cap. The cap can be pressed: pointing at it lists that tower's tickets that need you and what each asks; pressing it opens the one, on its own screen in Deck, where the red banner says what to do, or with several brings the phase close with them at the top of its list and the first selected. On this page, which has no ticket screen, pressing it brings the phase close with that ticket selected.
- **Tickets with no feature lie flat at the front of their segment**, as tiles in the same marks. Finished phases stand outside the ring as solid blocks of their tickets. The core holds the tickets with no phase.
- **What is being worked on is lit.** The feature in focus sends a beam of light up from its tower, and its tasks at `doing` pulse. The next ticket has a white outline and a "next" tag. It is the snapshot's focus task when one is named. When the snapshot names none, as on both workspaces here, it is the focus feature's first task at `doing`, then its first at `backlog`. Focus is read from each workspace's SNAPSHOT at the commit its fixture was taken from: on Deck that is PHASE-0005 and FEAT-0028, next TASK-0154; on your-trainer PHASE-025 and FEAT-0136, next TASK-1057.
- **A phase opens as its segment brought close**, in place of the web. The camera flies to the segment, the other phases fade to their plinths, and each tower shows its feature's id at its foot. Each tower's what-needs-you is listed first in its group. The phase's tickets are listed on the right as the cockpit lists them, grouped by tower with the focus feature first. Pressing a row outlines its floor; pressing a floor selects its row.
- **The exit criteria are a collar round the outside of each phase's rim, one cell per criterion, lit when met** (Edwin asked on 2026-10-16 for a different design than the arch of cubes that stood over the towers). Because the collar is on the ring itself, every phase shows at rest how close it is to closing, from any angle, and nothing stands over the towers. A fully lit collar is a phase ready to close. A met cell stands a little higher and glows; an unmet one is a dark cell with an outline. Pointing at a cell names its criterion. Brought close, the collar says "exit criteria · 4 of 7 met" in front of the segment, and the criteria head the list beside it. A phase that writes its criteria as prose, which cannot be counted, has a dashed line instead of cells.
- **The camera turns the way the hand moves.** A drag to the right moves the ring's near edge right, as Deck's camera now does too ([[TASK-0162]]). Shift-drag or the right button pans, the wheel zooms, and Home with three views (from above, leaning, from the side) puts it back. Escape goes from a phase back to the ring, then home.

The page is [DES-0005-towers.html](DES-0005-towers.html). It holds every ticket of both fixtures exactly once: 395 on Deck in 29 towers, and 2,747 on your-trainer in 69 towers. It is written by `desktop/scripts/overview-towers.mjs`, which the plate script runs, and loads three.js 0.160.0 from cdnjs. Nothing in Deck uses it. The pictures were taken by `desktop/scripts/towers-capture.cjs`.

## Answers proposed to DES-0004's open questions

1. **A backlog phase** is marked with `kind: backlog` in its note. When the note says nothing, an id ending in 999 is read as backlog, so your-trainer's PHASE-999 works today.
2. **An open risk is hollow**, like any ticket not started, in the cockpit's risk orange; a mitigating one is drawn in progress. In the state colours it is amber and blue. A risk register that stays open while its hazard exists draws the core's risk ring as outlines, and that is the honest picture.
3. **Accepting a re-phase suggestion does not write.** It moves the tickets on screen for the session. Deck has asked the cockpit for a guarded write that sets `phase:` ([[REFERENCE-COCKPIT-ADOPTION]], "Requests to the cockpit").
4. **This work owns a phase of its own**, [[PHASE-0005-Overview]], worked ahead of the rest of Glass.
