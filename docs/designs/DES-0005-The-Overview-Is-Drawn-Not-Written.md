# The overview is drawn, not written

**Deck's overview is now seven screens that show progress as pictures, and each one is drawn by the code Deck runs.** The wheel of phases opens Deck. Behind it are a phase, a ticket and re-phasing. Three more screens are where a person accepts a design, records an acceptance test and accepts a feature. Every plate below was drawn by the same functions Deck uses, from the real tickets of Deck and of your-trainer, so the design and the build cannot disagree. Edwin is asked to accept or decline it.

The interactive page is [DES-0005-the-overview.html](DES-0005-the-overview.html). It switches between the two workspaces and the seven screens, and pointing at any mark shows what it is. Served by the cockpit's viewer, a press on a sector, a ticket or a gate opens that screen, as in Deck. It is generated by `desktop/scripts/overview-plates.mjs`; nothing in it is drawn by hand.

> [!note] Revised and redrawn, 2026-10-15
> Edwin ran the built overview on 2026-10-15 and asked for five changes (under Revisions). The plates below are redrawn from the revised screens. Where a screen lists tickets as rows, the plate draws the rows flat; the pictures marked "in Deck" are taken from a real Deck window, where the rows stand on a tilted plane.

## Problem

**The Glass screens in DES-0004 show tickets as cards with titles, so reading them means reading text.** Plate 6 there shows 13 cards with truncated titles and an exit-criteria card of eight sentences. Plate 7 is a document, a list of features and a table of tests. Edwin's verdict on 2026-10-14: too text heavy, and nothing like Minority Report.

DES-0004's wheel works the other way. A glance says which phase is mostly done and where the open work is, and it carries no sentence. This design draws every screen that way, including three nobody had drawn: accepting a design, running an acceptance test and accepting a ticket.

**After using it, Edwin wanted tickets to look the same as in the cockpit, and to read them where reading is the point** (2026-10-15). A phase's tickets and a ticket's neighbours are lists, so they are drawn as the cockpit's rows, with an id, a title and a status each. A ticket's own screen shows its text.

## The look

The rules are in [[PHASE-0005-Overview]], "The look". Four of them are checked by code on every screen, on both workspaces, in `desktop/src/shared/overview-checks.ts`: at most 60 words at rest outside a list of tickets, every count drawn by its own mark, an address that names its screen, and a list twin that holds every drawn ticket and every control. The word count under each plate is that check's.

- **Colour is the ticket's type, as in the cockpit.** Features are blue, tasks green, requirements purple, issues red, risks orange, tests a lighter green, decisions teal and designs grey-blue. These are the cockpit's own colours (`--type-*` in its `cockpit.css`).
- **The fill is the state, as in the cockpit's overview.** Filled is done. Hollow is not started. Inverted and pulsing is in progress. Inverted and still is "complete, not proven". Solid with a slit is "resolved, nothing delivered". Hollow with a strike is "parked". A red dot on the corner is "needs you", whatever the type. On a wedge of the wheel, which is not a square, the same states are drawn as a full, hollow, half or dashed wedge, and "needs you" as a red outline that glows. The fill and the dot are worked out by the cockpit's own rules, ported into Deck and checked against the cockpit's answers: no ticket differs on either workspace ([[TASK-0144]]).
- **The state colours are still there to compare.** On the wheel and the re-phase screen, the ◐ button beside the list button, or the C key, redraws the wheel, its shelf and tray, and the re-phase cloud in DES-0004's state colours: closed green, in progress blue, failed red, owed violet, open amber, dropped grey. Lists of tickets, a ticket's own square and the gate keep the cockpit's marks in both. The colouring is part of the screen's address (`colour=state`), so a copied address shows the same picture; Deck remembers the last choice only for opening the wheel.
- **Shape is kind on the wheel and in the re-phase cloud.** Requirements are diamonds, features hexagons, tasks circles, tests squares, issues triangles, decisions and designs pentagons, risks octagons. In a list every ticket is the cockpit's square.
- **The instrument.** A dark field with a faint grid, corner brackets, a ticked bezel, and a scan ring that turns once a minute on the wheel. A list of tickets stands on a plane tilted away in real depth and leans a little toward the pointer. Moving between screens animates every ticket that is on both. Nothing moves for a person who asked for less motion.
- **Words appear on demand.** Pointing at a mark or a row shows its callout in the cockpit's words, for example "FEAT-0024 Evidence stands beside the claim it supports (review) — needs you". L opens the list twin: the same screen as rows, for the keyboard and a screen reader.
- **Back and forward.** ← and → beside the Deck name, ⌘[ and ⌘], and the mouse's own buttons retrace the screens visited, as a browser does. Escape still goes up a level, from a ticket to its phase and from a phase to the wheel.

## Answers proposed to DES-0004's open questions

1. **A backlog phase** is marked with `kind: backlog` in its note. When the note says nothing, an id ending in 999 is read as backlog, so your-trainer's PHASE-999 works today.
2. **An open risk is hollow**, like any ticket not started, in the cockpit's risk orange; a mitigating one is drawn in progress. In the state colours it is amber and blue. A risk register that stays open while its hazard exists draws the core's risk ring as outlines, and that is the honest picture.
3. **Accepting a re-phase suggestion does not write.** It moves the tickets on screen for the session. Deck has asked the cockpit for a guarded write that sets `phase:` ([[REFERENCE-COCKPIT-ADOPTION]], "Requests to the cockpit").
4. **This work owns a phase of its own**, [[PHASE-0005-Overview]], worked ahead of the rest of Glass.

## What is Edwin's to decide

- Accept or decline this design.
- Whether S5's fanned plates should be drawn as outlines only: behind a busy plate they compete with it.
- Whether the shelf's owed-again badge should open the release test, as it does now, or the finished phase.
- Which colouring the overview opens in. It opens in the cockpit's type colours, which Edwin asked to try; ◐ or C switches to the state colours, and Deck remembers the choice on that machine. Both wheels are drawn above.
- Whether two greens are acceptable. The cockpit's colours for tasks and for tests are both green, so on the wheel their two rings are told apart by place more than by colour.
- Whether an acceptance test that failed in the release ledger should need you. The cockpit's rule reads only the note's status, so your-trainer's TST-0656, which failed on Android, carries no red dot, and PHASE-021 shows nobody needed. DES-0004 counted it. Following the cockpit was the point of this revision, so a change belongs in the cockpit's rule.
